0.3.4 (the test build 0.3.4~dev2 with its version number changed)
Found on an iPhone with 0.3.4~dev1, fixed in 0.3.4~dev2
- Subtitle add-ons: the playing item now carries its IMDb number (the film's
  own; for an episode the show's). Wizdom asks the player for it. Without it
  Wizdom looked the film up by title at a second service and showed an empty
  list whenever that lookup failed. 0.3.3 had the same gap. With the number,
  Wizdom listed subtitles for the test film in 0.9 s on the phone.
- Service restart: switching the add-on off and on, or updating it, shortly
  after playback left it dead until Kodi was restarted ("Address already in
  use"). The stopping service closed its swarm first and its local API port
  last, Kodi ends a stopping script after five seconds wherever it stands,
  and the new instance gave up at the first refusal. Now the port is given
  back first; everything is told to stop before anything is waited for; the
  waiting is bounded; the new instance retries a busy port and tells the
  user after 20 s instead of dying; a service that could not start closes
  its threads.
- Stopping a film waits at most two seconds for dial attempts that are still
  timing out (ten before). A cancelled or failed preparation no longer holds
  the engine for up to ten more seconds, so the next source starts sooner.
- The listener watchdog cannot reopen the stream of a session that ended,
  one slow accept no longer makes it replace a healthy listener, and a
  rebuild that fails half-way leaves no bound port that nobody serves.
Independent review of that fix
- Round one, four reviewers and then skeptics: 11 findings, all confirmed or
  plausible, none refuted, all fixed with a test each (tests/test_restart.py).
- Round two, three reviewers on those fixes: nothing wrong in playback, 13
  low-severity findings (one a medium gap in the tests). Fixed: responses of
  a stream that was closed before the service stops, a listener closed under
  a running accept loop when no helper thread could start, a full disk that
  kept downloads running at shutdown, a read-ahead thread that failed to
  start, a web seed that answers nonsense; three test gaps closed. Left: a
  download ended by Kodi's forced end before the shutdown reached it is
  saved as failed with no text, and four gaps in the tests. Two of the three
  skeptics of this round were stopped before they finished.
  The ones that mattered most:
  - Kodi's forced end (SystemExit, once per thread) could land inside the
    shutdown and skip the steps after it. Every step now runs whatever
    happened to the one before, and a last pass repeats them without waiting.
  - Read-ahead threads of concurrent.futures stay blocked for ever when the
    service is ended by force. They are replaced by threads that end by
    themselves once the session is cancelled.
  - A response to a paused player sat in sendall() until the player read
    again. Writes are taken in one-second steps, and a stopping service drops
    its open responses. An ordinary stop still lets the player finish what
    is cached; a switch to another source relies on that.
  - A play request that was waiting for the resume question could start a
    session after the service had closed. A closed engine refuses it.
  - A cancelled piece went on to the HTTP web seeds of a .torrent source.
- Known limit: tracker announces and dial attempts that are in flight when
  the service stops cannot be interrupted and can outlive it by a few
  seconds. Kodi may then log "script didn't stop in 5 seconds"; the port is
  already free, the state is saved, and the next instance starts right after.
Measured on the iPhone (regular Kodi 21.3, Sintel only)
- 0.3.4~dev1, started through Kodi's remote interface: first moving frame
  7.0 s after the request (5.0 s of it preparation); duration and bitrate
  read from the file; start buffer 1.2 MiB in 0.5 s; seek index found; a
  seek to 60% played again after 2.4 s; 15 of 18 peers unchoked at 5 MiB/s;
  playback advanced 15.1 s in 15 s; free memory fell by 70 to 115 MB while
  playing with a 32 MiB cache; the menus answered after every stop.
- Service restart with the first version of the fix, before the review: back
  1.0 s after an idle switch, 1.9 s after a switch during playback and 5.6 s
  after a switch right after a stop, where Kodi still logged its forced end
  for lingering threads. "Address already in use" did not occur again. The
  review fixes have not run on a device.
- A weak source is still slow: two starts took 25-36 s. In the one that was
  examined, the first source in the list failed to deliver metadata (10 s)
  and the next one delivered about 80 KiB/s.
- 0.3.4~dev2 was then installed on the iPhone from its ZIP file. Its user
  reports that playback and Wizdom subtitles work; nothing was measured.
- Not yet observed on a device: a forced stall (the trickle and the
  same-position resume), leaving Kodi and returning, 8-16 MiB pieces, a full
  film, Wizdom on an episode.
Peers and scheduling
- Download each piece block by block from every unchoked peer at once. Before,
  one whole piece came from one peer and at most four pieces moved at a time.
- Serve the piece a reader waits for first; pipeline each peer by its measured
  rate; take over blocks that wait deep in another peer's queue; request an
  overdue urgent block a second time; snub a silent peer instead of waiting.
- Keep idle peers. The old reader dropped every peer that sent nothing for ten
  seconds and never dialed it again, so a swarm shrank to its busiest peers.
- Up to 20 connections while streaming (setting, 4-40) instead of 8; send
  keep-alives; dial a peer that delivered data again after a drop.
- Replace a peer that stays choked, stays silent or has none of the wanted
  pieces when a new candidate is waiting; keep peers that only miss one piece.
- Trace a failed piece to the peer whose block differs from the verified
  piece and ban only that peer. Every delivered byte is still SHA-1 verified.
- BEP 6 fast extension (HAVE_ALL/HAVE_NONE, explicit rejects, allowed-fast),
  PEX IPv6 peers and flags.
Cache and reading
- Fix eviction that discarded read-ahead before it was read: once the cache was
  full, every piece was downloaded almost four times (200 pieces, 755 fetches).
- Keep the first and last piece in a cache of 16 pieces or more.
- Follow the reader that is really moving when Kodi parks a connection during
  a seek; stop treating the parked reader's piece as urgent.
- Measure throughput by wall clock, so parallel pieces are not counted twice.
- No second copy of a piece on its way to the player (memory views).
Start and seek
- Read the duration from the Matroska/MP4 header: about eight seconds of this
  video are buffered before playback (setting, 0-16 MiB cap), less when the
  swarm is clearly faster than the video. Progress shows percent, rate, peers.
- Find a seek index stored after the media (MP4 moov, Matroska Cues) and fetch
  it while the start buffer fills.
- Move read-ahead to the new position when a seek starts, not after its first
  piece, and cancel read-ahead that the seek left behind.
- Tell Kodi the stream's MIME type: two HEAD probes and one discarded GET less.
Discovery
- Thirteen built-in public trackers that each answered two announces on
  2026-11-16, added to the source's own trackers (never for a private torrent).
- Use each tracker's and each DHT node's peers the moment they answer; try 32
  peers for metadata at once; five DHT bootstrap routers; remember DHT nodes
  between sessions; reuse verified metadata saved by an earlier session.
- UDP trackers fall back to IPv6 when IPv4 has no route (NAT64 networks).
Stalls and recovery
- Hold back 64 KiB of verified data and release 256 bytes a second during a
  stall. Kodi ends playback after about ten seconds without a byte once its
  cache is empty; with the trickle it keeps buffering and recovery can act.
- Reopen the same live session at the same position when Kodi ends playback
  far from the end without a user stop (at most twice per title).
- Reopen the local listeners if iOS reclaimed them while Kodi was suspended.
- Stable failure reasons in diagnostics and plain advice for the viewer.
Independent review before packaging
- Four reviewers read the new code by area and a skeptic then tried to refute
  or reproduce each report: 25 defects confirmed, 1 plausible, none refuted.
  All 26 are fixed and each has a regression test (tests/test_review_fixes.py).
  The ones that mattered most:
  - A BEP 6 peer that chokes must reject every queued request. Those rejects
    were counted as misbehaviour, so an ordinary libtorrent-family seeder was
    set aside after its first upload-slot rotation and never used again.
  - A fast peer that went silent kept slower healthy peers away from the last
    blocks of the awaited piece, which then never completed.
  - The single-peer retry after a hash failure stalled when that peer was the
    slower one, or when it merely choked.
  - One peer that stopped reading its socket could hold up every scheduler
    thread for up to 150 seconds; replies a peer can solicit are now bounded.
  - A crafted Matroska header made the duration reader quadratic (hours).
  - A broken reply from one HTTP tracker aborted the whole magnet lookup.
  - Two connections waiting for the same piece could cancel each other; a
    closed connection restarted the piece it had just given up; the read-ahead
    window did not return to an older connection that Kodi resumed.
  - A rebuilt listener left its old accept loop spinning on a dead socket.
Tools and tests
- tools/swarm_bench.py: scripted loopback swarm with real rates and faults,
  Kodi-like reader, comparison of two engine versions.
- tools/real_swarm_check.py, tools/package_addon.py, tools/validate_package.py.
- 252 tests pass against the packaged code (124 in 0.3.3), no skips.
- Two tests were rewritten because the mechanism they pinned was replaced
  (reserved upload slot, whole-piece fake peer); one test double gained keyword
  arguments. All other 0.3.3 tests are unchanged.
- Not done: uTP, MSE/PE encryption, BitTorrent v2, upload. A real swarm showed
  1 of 140 failed dials closing after a plaintext handshake, so encryption
  would add little; the engine now reports that figure and the PEX share.
